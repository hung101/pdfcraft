Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert PDF pages to images securely?
Drop your PDF into our converter, choose your format (JPG or PNG) and image quality, and click Convert. The pages are rendered locally via browser canvas rendering and saved as an image ZIP bundle.
Can I extract individual pages as images?
Yes, the tool converts each individual page into a separate image file and packages them in a ZIP archive.
What resolution are the output images?
You can choose between standard screen resolution (150 DPI) or print quality (300 DPI) for your output image frames.
Are my documents processed on a server?
No. PDFCraft uses client-side rendering engines (pdf.js) to draw the document pages locally in your browser memory.

Rendering pages the way a viewer would

Each page is handed to a PDF rendering engine, drawn onto a canvas at the resolution you choose, and then exported as a PNG or JPEG. What you get is what a PDF reader would have shown you on screen, captured at your chosen size. When you export more than one page, the images are gathered into a ZIP archive that is also assembled in the browser.

Resolution is expressed in DPI because PDF pages are measured in points, at 72 per inch. Selecting 150 DPI renders each page at roughly twice its nominal pixel size; 300 DPI at about four times, and so on.

Picking a format and a resolution

  • PNG is lossless and large; JPEG is lossy and small. Use PNG for diagrams, screenshots and anything with sharp text edges. Use JPEG for photographic pages where file size matters more.
  • Memory grows with the square of DPI. Doubling the resolution quadruples the pixels. Long documents at 600 DPI can exhaust the browser tab.
  • The output contains no text. Converting a page to an image discards the characters; there is no OCR step to put them back.
  • Transparency is not preserved. Pages render against their own background, so PNG output is opaque.

Getting usable images out

What DPI should I use for printing?
Use 300 DPI, which is the long-standing print standard. For viewing on a screen, 150 DPI is usually plenty and produces files a quarter of the size.
Can I convert only certain pages?
Yes. Select the pages you want before exporting, and only those are rendered. This is worth doing on long documents, since rendering is the slow part.
Why is my ZIP file so much larger than the PDF?
Because a PDF stores instructions and a PNG stores pixels. A page described in a few kilobytes of text and vectors becomes a full-resolution bitmap. Choosing JPEG, or a lower DPI, brings the size down.
