Private, Browser-Based PDF Tools

Local Processing

Unlike other online PDF converters, PDFCraft processes all your files directly in your web browser. Your sensitive documents are never uploaded to any remote server.

No Server Copy

Because there is no upload step, your document never reaches a server we control, so there is no stored copy to leak. The site does run Google Analytics, which records anonymous visits and which tools are used but never receives your file; the security page lists exactly what it sees. Keeping files off servers removes the transfer step that usually causes the difficulty under GDPR, HIPAA or corporate confidentiality rules, though it is not a compliance certification.

Works Offline

Your first visit saves every tool in your browser, so they keep working without an internet connection. Only the typeface and the analytics need the network, and no tool depends on either.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PDFCraft really free to use?
Yes. PDFCraft is free, with no subscription, no account, and no PDFCraft watermark stamped on anything you download. There is no file size limit beyond what your own device's memory can handle. The site carries no advertising: hosting is paid out of pocket, with voluntary donations helping to offset it.
How can I edit my PDFs offline?
Open PDFCraft once while you're online and give it a few seconds: it saves every tool in your browser, and after that they work without an internet connection. Installing it is optional. Chrome and Edge offer an install button in the address bar; Safari has Add to Home Screen on iPhone and Add to Dock on Mac.
Are my documents uploaded to a server?
No. All file processing, rendering, and downloading happen entirely inside your browser tab on your local CPU. Your files are never sent to a backend server or stored anywhere online.
Can I use PDFCraft with GDPR- or HIPAA-sensitive documents?
Because PDFCraft processes everything locally and never uploads, stores, or transmits your documents, no third-party processor ever receives them — which is usually the step that creates a GDPR or HIPAA problem. That removes the external-transfer risk, but it is not a compliance certification and nothing here is legal advice: whether a given workflow meets your obligations is a decision for your own compliance team.
