Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert JPG images to PDF offline?
Select or drag-and-drop your image files into our converter, choose your page orientation and margin preferences, and click Convert. The compilation runs client-side and downloads the PDF instantly.
Which image formats are supported?
PDFCraft supports converting JPG, JPEG, PNG, and WebP image formats.

JPEGs go in without a second round of compression

A JPEG you add is embedded into the PDF as its original compressed bytes. It is not decoded to pixels and re-encoded on the way in, which means it does not pick up a second generation of JPEG artefacts. The photo inside the finished PDF is byte-for-byte the photo you selected.

PNG files are embedded as lossless image data, so line art, screenshots and diagrams keep their crisp edges. Each image is then scaled to fit the page size, orientation and margin you chose, and drawn at the alignment you picked.

Before you convert a folder of photos

  • Large photos stay large. Nothing is downsampled to fit the page, so twelve 8-megapixel images produce a PDF roughly the size of twelve 8-megapixel images.
  • HEIC and HEIF are not supported. Browsers cannot decode them natively. Convert to JPEG first, which iPhones can do on export.
  • Transparency is composited. A PNG with a transparent background is drawn onto the page, so the transparent area becomes the page colour rather than staying transparent.
  • The result is pictures, not text. A PDF built from photographs of documents is not searchable. There is no OCR step here.

Page setup and image handling

Does converting to PDF reduce my photo quality?
JPEG input is embedded without re-encoding, so no quality is lost on the way in. What changes is presentation: the image is scaled to the page size you selected, so a very large photo on a small page is displayed smaller while retaining its full stored detail.
Can I mix JPEG and PNG files in one document?
Yes. Each image is embedded in the form appropriate to its type, and both sit happily in the same file.
How do I control which image lands on which page?
Each image becomes one page, in the order shown in the list. Rearrange the list before exporting and the page order follows.
